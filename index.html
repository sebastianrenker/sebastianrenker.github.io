Infrastructure for autonomous AI

Trusted infrastructure for autonomous AI.

AI systems are becoming capable of acting — operating a computer, sending messages, running experiments. RENKER builds the layer that makes those actions controllable, verifiable, and accountable.

Capability shouldn't imply permission. The decision of what an agent may do lives in a small, deterministic, auditable layer outside the model — one that reads only trusted grants, never the agent's own claims about what it should be allowed to do.

§ stance

What RENKER is — and isn't

The discipline is deliberate: it matches what the repositories actually state about themselves.

It is

  • Infrastructure for one narrow, real problem: making autonomous AI actions controllable, verifiable, accountable.
  • Explainable — every decision a RENKER component makes should be inspectable, not a black box.
  • Honest about maturity: prototypes are called prototypes, private repos are called private.

It is not

  • An AGI project, or any implication that these systems approach general intelligence.
  • "Unhackable" or perfectly secure — every project ships a documented security model with known limits.
  • A claim to revolutionize an industry. It solves a specific problem, well.
§ foundation

The shared foundation

Every RENKER project answers the same questions — who is acting, what are they allowed to do, who decided that, and can you prove what happened. Those answers live in one place instead of being reinvented per project.

renker-core · shared foundation

Identity, capability, policy, audit

The primitives Rencora, RenkerVault, and Continuum all consume: who is acting (human, agent, service, device), what they hold as a scoped, revocable capability, the deterministic policy that turns a request into a decision, and a tamper-evident record of it.
